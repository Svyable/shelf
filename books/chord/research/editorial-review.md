# CHORD — Editorial and provenance review

**Review date:** November 16, 2026. **Review status:** First complete manuscript assembled and checked for structural, quantitative, metadata, and source-status consistency. This is **not** independent peer review or external scientific fact-checking, and the book remains open to editorial refinement.

## Concrete editorial decisions

1. **Opening versus conclusion:** Chapter 1 begins with two rival images whose physical presentation continues despite a changing percept. Chapter 18 returns to the distinction among representation, causal access and subjective experience. Neither the opening nor the conclusion claims to have measured machine experience.
2. **Different clocks, different tasks:** Chapters 2–5 distinguish an early visual response, stimulus-specific macaque code switching under 20 ms, human recurrent feature-binding dynamics around 120 ms, and a bioRxiv report of ~15 Hz alternation. They are deliberately not harmonized into a universal cycle.
3. **Global access and biology:** Chapters 6–10 separate adversarial testing of GNWT/IIT, human thalamic interventions, masked inference, TMS/EEG perturbation, and fitted clinical neural-field models. Clinical measures are not generalized into AI consciousness scores.
4. **Negative results are kept central:** Chapter 13 preserves the high-noise failure of the raw novelty gate, the reliability-normalization repair, and the distinction between sample timing and intrinsic phase. Chapter 14 preserves the unchanged gate-ablation result, uncertain paired identity comparison, and reversed-distractor collapse.
5. **Architecture is explicitly contestable:** Chapters 15–17 require equal samples and resources, separately retrained comparators, representation geometry, targeted perturbations, and Bayesian/symbolic/generic recurrent rivals. Chapter 18 preserves the contested philosophical question.
6. **Hypothetical cases labeled:** Robots moving packages, corridor navigation and simulated observations are conceptual examples and future benchmark proposals, not claims of completed field research.
7. **Reader-facing rights:** All Rights Reserved text and machine-readable rights copied from the active Desk book standard; no third-party quotations or figures have been reproduced beyond the book's short descriptions and links.

## Known editorial and scientific limitations

- The manuscript was composed in one integrated generation rather than through independent substantive reviewer rounds. Length and automated checks alone do not prove literary quality. A later line edit can reduce recurring transition language, but should not remove the critical negative findings.
- The original TACH executable research bundle is retained in the author's prior research files; the public reader receives a detailed provenance account, not raw zipped source code or a replication certificate.
- TACH-6's 902-parameter matching does not equal formal FLOP matching. Identity performance remains weak; the experiment was exploratory. No direct neural recordings were analyzed by the artificial laboratory.
- Hesse and Bennett were preprints at this evidence cutoff; the Butlin report is a theoretical indicator framework; the cognitive architecture is a proposal, not proof.
- The Cóngyǔ article was initially labeled an uncorrected proof by its publisher. A later corrected version may refine numerical language or interpretation.
- DOI-backed primary articles and original reports are linked in the prose and ledger; no exhaustive systematic review or external expert audit is claimed.
