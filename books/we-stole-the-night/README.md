## Premise

We made nighttime illumination cheaper, more reliable, and almost effortless. In doing so, we changed not only cities and working hours but the ecological conditions of sleep, migration, pollination, reproduction, and navigation. The problem is not that every lamp is harmful. It is that light travels farther than the task it was purchased to serve.

Beginning with the October 2023 mass bird collision at Chicago’s McCormick Place and its subsequent bird-visible window treatment, this book follows artificial light from 1807 gaslit London to 2026 research on birds, bats, lakes, insects, and cities. It takes both the benefits of public lighting and the costs of light spill seriously, treating measurement, direction, spectrum, timing, and governance as the decisive design variables.

**Reader promise:** A research-driven account of what we gained from the second sun, what became harder to see, and how real places can recover useful darkness without sacrificing legitimate nighttime needs.
