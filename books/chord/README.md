## Premise

Conscious thinking looks continuous even though the neural mechanisms of attention, memory, representation and recurrence operate at different timescales. CHORD asks what a machine must actually do to select information, bind it, preserve a causal account of the world and distinguish its own actions from external changes.

The book puts primary neuroscience beside original, unpublished TACH synthetic experiments, including negative findings: novelty gating failed under noisy observations; a learned code-remapping event was not causally validated. The proposal is a falsifiable architecture for integrated cognition, not a claim that software has demonstrated subjective experience.

**Reader promise:** follow the evidence from binocular rivalry through thalamic gating, recurrent binding, clinical perturbation, and a six-generation artificial laboratory, and leave with exact experiments capable of disproving the proposed mechanisms.
