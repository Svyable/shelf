# Back Matter

## What has—and has not—been established

The 2026 neuroscience findings described in this book concern measurable neural activity, behavioral responses, and theoretical interpretations of information access. They do not provide a validated method for detecting subjective experience in a computer.

The TACH experiments described in Chapters 11–15 are exploratory software studies using constructed environments. They have not been independently replicated on biological neural recordings. Their results should not be treated as empirical evidence that an artificial system is conscious. The negative and inconclusive results have been retained because the architecture must remain falsifiable.

CHORD is a proposed computational arrangement, not a demonstrated necessary-and-sufficient architecture of mind. It may prove useful for organizing local processing, global selection, recurrent binding, and persistent causal modeling. Each component requires separate comparison against strong alternatives.

## About the laboratory

TACH—the Temporal Access and Continuity Hypothesis—was explored through successive synthetic computational experiments. CHORD names a subsequent proposed design for coordinated, temporally persistent cognitive computation. The research trail contains the decision ledger, source chronology, and details of the unpublished simulation outcomes, including failures.

The book does not license a consciousness classification from those outcomes. In particular, the TACH-6 model's numerical identity advantage was not reliable against every matched comparator, did not survive the stipulated causal interpretation test, and was accompanied by serious distractor-shift weaknesses.
